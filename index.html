The tools

Two different jobs. One finds out why your server died. The other finds the things that are broken and never say so.

Forge Server Doctor

$19 · free edition available

Reads a crash report or latest.log and reports the root cause, the mod responsible, and the fix.

  • 24 failure modes, each with its own written remedy
  • Names the mod jar at fault from the stack trace
  • Verified against 13 real crash reports from a live 234-mod server
  • --json and real exit codes for start scripts

Pack Doctor

$24

Indexes every ID your mods actually register, then checks everything your datapacks, KubeJS scripts, quests and configs reference against it.

  • Finds typo'd IDs that silently give the player nothing
  • Knows KubeJS startup scripts register content
  • Findings tiered by confidence, so it doesn't cry wolf
  • Flags duplicate mod jars before they stop your server booting

Found in a real, widely-played pack

Built against real data, not invented samples

The first version of Forge Server Doctor passed every test I wrote and still missed five of eight real crash reports — the same person had written both the samples and the patterns. It got rebuilt against an actual crash-report folder.

Pack Doctor went the same way: its first run reported 169 typos on a real pack and most were noise, because comparing whole IDs lets a shared namespace inflate every similarity score. Comparing the paths alone cut it to 63 real candidates.

Both tools ship their test suites. Every test is mutation-checked — the fix reverted, the test confirmed failing — so a passing suite means something.

What they will not do

A diagnostic tool that overstates its reach wastes more of your time than one that admits its edges.

  • They read files, not your game. They cannot know two mods conflict unless the log says so.
  • A native JVM crash leaves nothing to parse. No Java trace, no crash report — look for an hs_err_pid file instead.
  • When nothing matches, they say so and tell you where to look by hand, instead of inventing a diagnosis. "No known pattern" is not "nothing is wrong".
  • They can be wrong. Back up your world before acting on anything that deletes configs or drops registry entries.
  • Built and verified on 1.20.1. The patterns are largely version-independent and NeoForge is handled, but 1.21+ output can differ.

Forge Server Tools — diagnostic aids, not authorities. Verify any diagnosis before acting on it, and keep a backup of your world.
